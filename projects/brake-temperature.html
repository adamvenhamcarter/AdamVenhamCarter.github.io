IR Brake Rotor Temperature System

A non-contact sensing concept for measuring brake-rotor surface temperature in real time during endurance racing.

IR TemperatureThermal DesignPackagingValidation

Problem

Brake temperature is directly tied to performance, wear, and component safety, but measuring a rotating rotor on-track requires non-contact sensing in a harsh thermal and vibration environment.

Requirements

  • Target measurement accuracy within ±10%.
  • Survive a full 12-hour endurance event.
  • Measure the rotor wear surface without interfering with brake hardware.
  • Begin with the front axle and preserve serviceability.

Thermal and packaging approach

The concept places the sensor on or near the brake shroud with line-of-sight to the rotor friction surface. Thermal analysis is used to evaluate whether the local sensor environment remains within hardware limits when rotor temperatures are substantially higher.

Show the decision process: include the estimated rotor temperature, predicted surrounding/shield temperature, chosen sensor range, standoff distance, and why the mounting geometry is acceptable.

Validation plan

Bench validation should compare the IR reading against a trusted reference across representative temperatures before track use. Track validation can then focus on signal stability, repeatability, wiring survivability, and whether the data responds coherently to braking events.
