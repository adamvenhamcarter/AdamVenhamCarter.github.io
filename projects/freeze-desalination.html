Freeze Desalination Research

Experimental investigation of freeze desalination, ice formation, heat transfer, and ice adhesion with an emphasis on translating physical behavior into a controllable engineering process.

Heat TransferExperimental DesignANSYS FluentMATLABInstrumentation

The engineering problem

Freeze desalination separates fresh water by selectively freezing water while rejecting dissolved salt. My work examined the thermal and mechanical behavior involved in producing and removing ice from a cooled surface.

Experimental approach

The research used an aluminum helical-coil heat exchanger with controlled sub-zero coolant conditions. Testing examined ice growth under water layers and the interaction between ice, surface treatment, flow, and temperature.

  • Controlled cold-side temperature during freezing experiments.
  • Evaluated heat-transfer and fluid-flow effects on ice formation.
  • Used hydrophobic surface treatment and controlled surface preparation.
  • Processed experimental measurements for comparison across test conditions.

Modeling and mechanics

Alongside testing, I worked on analytical descriptions of the forces involved in ice removal and used dimensional reasoning to identify the variables governing mechanical response. CFD analysis was also used to understand flow behavior around the test geometry.
